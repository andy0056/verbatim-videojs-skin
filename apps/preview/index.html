Video.js v10 skin·Verbatim

The subtitle became the player.

The sentence you are hearing is the control bar. Words fill as they are spoken. Drag along the words to scrub; click a word to jump straight to it.

Open in the workbench

Playing How are we made of star stuff? from NASA's image and video library, streamed from Mux with Mux's auto-generated captions. The player works from the WebVTT alone; there are no word-level timestamps.

Read it

Spoken words are bright, upcoming words are dim, and the current word carries a highlighter stroke with a progress underline.

Scrub it

Press on any word and drag. Cross the end of the sentence to flip to the next one. Click a word to jump to it.

Preview it

Point at the thin seek bar and the sentence spoken at that moment takes the stage, set as a quotation, with its time and chapter above. No popup over the picture.

Key it

Tab to the sentence: ← → step by word, ↑ ↓ by sentence, Home and End. The thin track is a standard time slider with chapter segments; J, K, L, M, F, C and the numbers work as usual.

Why fold captions into the controls?

Every player treats captions as a layer over the picture and then lays the controls on top of that layer. Two surfaces compete for the same strip of screen, and the words a viewer is actually reading are the ones that get covered when a hand moves toward the seek bar.

Verbatim makes them one surface. The sentence sits where the controls go, so the thing you read is the thing you steer with. Seeking stops being a guess along a thin line and becomes go back to where she said “analogy”. For a video podcast, a lecture, an interview in a museum archive, or a founder talking through a case study on a portfolio, that is how people already think about the material.

It has to stay a player other people can drop into their sites, so the conventions hold: a visible seek track, standard play, volume, captions and fullscreen glyphs, menus that grow out of their buttons, and a layout that collapses sensibly on a phone. The reading plate is translucent in its colour rather than its opacity, so text stays opaque over bright and dark footage, and it turns solid when a viewer asks for more contrast or less transparency. With no captions track at all, the plate shows the title and Verbatim behaves like a quiet, conventional skin.

The skin runs on Video.js 10's own components. The one new part is <verbatim-transcript>, which reads the first captions track, merges Mux-style fragment cues into sentences, times each word by its share of its cue, and exposes itself to assistive technology as a slider whose value text is the current word and time.

Craft notes

  • Colour carries the transparency. Surfaces use dark colour at partial alpha, never opacity, so glyphs and text keep full contrast.
  • Concentric radii. The plate radius is the control radius plus the plate padding, so corners sit evenly.
  • Tabular numerals wherever digits tick, including the eyebrow and the time readout.
  • Own type. A book serif for the sentence, a UI sans for everything else; the host page's font is never inherited. Fallbacks are system serifs, and nothing is downloaded by the skin.
  • Motion with restraint. Words switch state in 150 ms; menus open from their triggers and close faster than they open. prefers-reduced-motion removes the transitions.
  • Preferences respected. prefers-contrast: more and prefers-reduced-transparency make the plate solid; forced-colors maps the highlighter to the system Highlight.
  • Public knobs. --media-accent-color, --media-accent-text-color, --media-border-radius, --media-font-family and --media-scale-unit work as on the packaged skins, plus --vb-sentence-font and --vb-plate.
